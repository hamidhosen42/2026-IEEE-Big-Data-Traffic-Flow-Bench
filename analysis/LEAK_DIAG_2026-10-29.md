> **DIAGNOSTIC — NOT FOR SUBMISSION.** This analysis reads Task 2 data timestamped **after the forecast origin T**:
> on-ramp flows inside the horizon, and mainline cells after the 60-minute buffer. Organizer rule 742068
> (`research/organizer_verified_facts.md`, item 1) puts both **out of scope** ("ramp values inside the horizon",
> "mainline observations after the buffer").
> - Nothing here may enter a submission, a Kaggle kernel or the consolidation code.
> - The scripts are `analysis/t2lab/leakdiag_*.py`. Each one carries the same banner, and no production file imports them.
> - Nothing was pushed or submitted, and no token file was touched.

**Question.** A public notebook (plarmuseau, *eleven anomalies*, section 2) says three things:
- on-ramps switch off when the adjacent mainline link jams;
- the released ramp files cover the Task 2 horizon;
- using this leak is worth +0.038 IoU on ongoing jams.

Can this leak, together with any other released post-T data, explain the jump of the top teams to about 0.93? Our public LB is 0.88225; the top is 0.92942.

## Bottom line

1. **No.** The leak is real and available in validation/private, but it is small.
   - **On pseudo-March** (new scenario, models at their LB-like level), the best leak reader adds:
     - horizon ramps only: **+0.0031** S_total;
     - ramps plus post-buffer mainline: **+0.0065** S_total;
     - against the no-leak control: at most +0.0082.
   - **On train official-like windows:** +0.0031 / +0.0032.
   - So 0.882 plus the gain gives **≈ 0.885–0.889**, not 0.93.
2. **The leak is almost useless for onset**, the condition where we lose most.
   - A true T+30 onset link carries an on-ramp in 74 % of windows (official 82 %).
   - But that ramp is switched off at T+30 in only 17 % (official 15 %).
   - Inside onset windows, 3 of 4 switched-off ramps sit on links that are still free at T+30 (flag precision 0.18–0.25).
   - Reason: the switch-off is a deep-jam effect (speed below about 0.5 × free speed). Onset queues at T+30 are shallow.
   - Measured onset gain: about 0 on train; +0.003 on pseudo-March (CI −0.028..+0.030).
3. **Ongoing gains are real but small:** +0.017 to +0.021 IoU, with CIs above 0, on train offlike, official-40 and pseudo-March. That is in the same range as the notebook's +0.038. It is worth only about +0.003 S_total.
4. **The simple leak rules hurt badly** (−0.09 to −0.34 IoU). The rule was "queued if the attached ramp is below 0.3 × its profile", optionally spread to neighbour links. Switched-off ramps are too often next to free links.
5. **Even oracles fall short.**

   | Oracle | S_total ceiling (pseudo-March) | S_total (0.882 +) |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | Perfect reader of every link with a usable on-ramp, at every step | +0.015 | 0.898 |
   | Truth on every link within ±2 links of a usable on-ramp (far beyond what the signal carries) | +0.042 | 0.924 |
   | A perfect Task 2 | +0.059 | 0.941 |

   To reach 0.929 through Task 2 alone, a team needs S_queue ≈ 0.94–0.95 on the LB (ours ≈ 0.79). That means near-perfect onset location *and* ongoing extent, which this channel cannot deliver.

## 2. How on-ramps react to the mainline

**Definition.** A ramp is "switched off" at a cell when flow ≤ 0.3 × its same-slot "on level". The on level is the 90th percentile over days of the same class (weekday / weekend), slots ±1. A cell counts only if that level is at least 10 vph. Statistics use daytime (06–21 h) cells where the attached link is observed.

Share of cells switched off, by the attached link's speed / free speed:

| speed / v_f | < 0.2 | 0.2–0.3 | 0.3–0.4 | 0.4–0.5 | 0.5–0.6 | 0.6–0.7 | 0.7–0.8 | ≥ 0.8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| train | 0.95 | 0.65 | 0.77 | 0.73 | **0.31** | 0.32 | 0.08 | 0.025 |
| March (validation) | 0.84 | 0.67 | 0.73 | 0.71 | **0.27** | 0.46 | 0.07 | 0.027 |
| April (private) | 0.96 | 0.70 | 0.79 | 0.72 | **0.29** | 0.37 | 0.27 | 0.029 |

- **The coupling is identical in all three splits**, so the leak exists in validation and private.
- It is a deep-jam signal: strong below 0.5 × v_f, weak in the 0.5–0.6 band where a queue (≤ 0.6 × v_f) starts.
- About 2.5 % of free cells are false flags. Free cells outnumber queued cells by about 13:1, so false flags matter.

Per panel, on train (`results/leakdiag/cell_stats.csv`):

| Quantity | Range |
|---|---|
| Usable on-ramp links | 44–59 per panel, i.e. 15–72 % of links (I-210 E, I-405 N and I-5 N are sparse) |
| P(link queued \| switched off) | 0.04–0.81 |
| P(switched off \| link queued) | 0.35–0.92 |

The ramp→link table learned from train confirms that a switch-off is co-located only loosely. The best-matching link is the attached one for only 2–12 % of ramps; the median offset is 3–10 links.

## 3. Method

**Windows.**
- `offlike`: official-like windows from the selection-faithful selector (t36-filled persistence cap), greedy restarted at every train day, 5 + 5 quota, 360-minute spacing. 2,105 onset and 2,992 ongoing windows.
- `official`: the 40 + 40 official train windows.
- Truth is the observed queue state on observed cells, from the lab row caches.

**Production-like base, out of fold** (4 day-block folds):
- Onset: CatBoost NORAMP, 600 it / depth 8 / lr 0.08, fold-safe priors, T+30 only. It is trained in `leakdiag_build.py` on dense1500 onset rows (offlike 0.894, official 0.946).
- Ongoing: the cached F03 np255 OOF probabilities, `fc_F03_np255` (offlike 0.875, official 0.890).

**Leak inputs** (`leakdiag_lib.py`):
- Ramp cubes at T+h and T+h±1:
  - own-ramp ratio and switch-off flag;
  - switched-off ramp counts within ±1 / ±2 / ±3 links;
  - fold-safe ramp→link tables P(Q_k | ramp a off), P(ramp a off | Q_k) and P(Q_k | ramp a on), which spread the signal to any link.
- Post-buffer mainline at T+95..T+105, thinned to the release visibility of 0.52:
  - queued or visible flags;
  - minimum speed ratio;
  - queued neighbours within ±2 links.

**Leak predictors** (same windows, same production expected-IoU decode):

| Name | What it does |
|---|---|
| `R_self` | p = max(p, 0.9) where the own on-ramp is switched off at T+h |
| `R_spread` | `R_self`, plus 0.6 within ±1 link and 0.4 within ±2 links |
| `R_map` | p = max(p, P(Q_k \| switched-off ramp)) from the learned table |
| `S_ctrl` | LightGBM stacker on (p, h, qlast) only: the no-leak control |
| `S_ramp` | stacker + ramp leak features |
| `S_pb` | stacker + post-buffer mainline features |
| `S_both` | stacker + both |

- The stackers boost a residual on the base logit (`init_score`). They are cross-fitted by fold, and official uses the fold-0 model.
- For pseudo-March, the stackers are trained on all train offlike cells and applied unchanged.

**Pseudo-March** (`leakdiag_pmarch.py`) uses the masked validation month, a new scenario.
- Onset: 214 windows, production NORAMP CatBoost with the s4 same-scenario prior (0.7875; reproduces `results/splitprior_noramp`).
- Ongoing: 298 `offlike_m` windows with the F03 base probabilities (0.8178).
- Leak inputs come from March itself: the ramp profile is computed on the March month, and the ramp→link tables come from train.
- Labels are the visible masked cells.

**Oracles** (`leakdiag_bounds.py`, `leakdiag_pmarch_bounds.py`): truth is substituted on the cells a leak could see; the base model is kept elsewhere.

- Onset links usually do have an on-ramp. The ramp seldom switches off at T+30, because the queue is still shallow.
- When some ramp in the window is off, it is usually not on the onset link.
- The post-buffer state does cover the onset link in 70–85 % of windows. But it is a much larger, later queue:
  - on its own (S_pb) it adds nothing to onset (train −0.005, pseudo-March −0.001);
  - combined with the ramps (S_both), pseudo-March onset shows +0.028, but the CI includes 0.

Ongoing (train offlike), for comparison:
- 14–15 % of true queued cells have their own ramp switched off;
- 8–13 % of new (growth) cells do;
- flag precision is 0.67–0.69.

## 7. Ceilings: the most any reader of these channels could gain

| Ceiling | train offlike: ΔIoU onset / ongoing | S_total | pseudo-March: ΔIoU onset / ongoing | S_total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| O_ramp: truth at every usable on-ramp cell | +0.029 / +0.028 | +0.0085 | +0.063 / +0.040 | **+0.0154** |
| O_ramp_pm2: truth at every link within ±2 of a usable ramp | +0.073 / +0.086 | +0.0238 | +0.149 / +0.129 | **+0.0418** |
| perfect Task 2 | +0.106 / +0.125 | +0.0346 | +0.213 / +0.182 | **+0.0592** |

- On train offlike, 39 % (onset) and 24 % (ongoing) of the base model's error cells sit on usable-ramp cells; 84 % and 66 % sit within ±2 links.
- Coverage of the oracles: usable on-ramp cells are 10–53 % of all horizon cells (mean 29 %); their ±2-link neighbourhoods are 44–97 % (mean 72 %).
- O_ramp_pm2 is not attainable. It assumes the ramp channel reveals the full state of about 72 % of all cells. The realized pseudo-March readers recover 7–16 % of it (S_ramp +0.0031, S_both +0.0065), and about 20 % of O_ramp.
- A post-buffer oracle is not shown, because it would cover 87 % of links, i.e. almost a perfect forecast. The realized post-buffer gain is in sections 4–5.

## 8. Verdict: 0.882 + gain vs 0.93

- **Our LB:** 0.88225, with LB S_queue ≈ 0.79. That estimate comes from the probe on 09-28 (onset 0.743, ongoing 0.747) plus the T2 gains since then. It also matches pseudo-March: 0.788 / 0.818.
- **Gap to 0.92942:** +0.0472. Through Task 2 alone that needs ΔS_queue = +0.157, i.e. S_queue ≈ 0.94–0.95.
- **Realized leak:** +0.003 (ramps) to +0.0065 (ramps + post-buffer), so **0.885–0.889**.
- **Ceilings:**
  - 0.898: perfect reading of the attached links;
  - 0.924: perfect reading of the ±2-link neighbourhoods, which this channel cannot provide;
  - 0.941: perfect Task 2.
- **Conclusion: 0.882 + gain ≠ 0.93.**
  - The horizon-ramp leak, alone or with the post-buffer mainline, explains at most about +0.003..+0.008 of the +0.047.
  - A post-rebuild 0.93 needs near-perfect onset location and ongoing extent, or large gains outside Task 2.
  - Neither follows from the channels measured here.
  - Public-LB selection noise (±0.003–0.02 for heavy submitters, `CAPACITY_SCENARIO_2026-10-11.md` §4) does not close the gap either.

## 9. Caveats

- **Truth proxy.** Truth is the observed queue state on observed cells: 76 % of cells in train, 52 % on pseudo-March. The organizer scores the noise-free state on all cells.
- **Pseudo-March windows** are lab-selected, not the official ones. The onset CIs are wide.
- **The stackers are simple:** residual LightGBM on the base logit, trained on offlike cells only. A model retrained from scratch with leak features might extract somewhat more. The O_ramp ceiling (+0.015) bounds any reader of attached-link states, and even the ±2-link oracle stays below the gap.
- **The post-buffer features use only T+95..T+105.** Using more of the later day cannot beat the perfect-Task-2 ceiling either.
- **Profile choice.** The ramp profile is taken from the same split, as a leaker would. Ramp flows were exported as float16; zeros are exact.
- **Version history** (the conclusion is the same in every version; S_total ≤ +0.0065 throughout):
  - v1, plain-tree stacker: piecewise-constant p created ties that the top-m decode broke arbitrarily, costing onset 0.012 even in the control. Logs: `logs/leakdiag_*_v1_plainstack.log`.
  - v2, median profile with a 50-vph floor: this dropped the recurrently-off and low-flow ramps, i.e. the informative cells. Logs: `logs/leakdiag_*_v2_medprof.log`, results `results/leakdiag/v2_medprof_*`.
  - v3 (above): p90 profile, 10-vph floor, residual stacker.
