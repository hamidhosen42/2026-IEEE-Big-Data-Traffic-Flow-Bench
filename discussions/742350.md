# Task 2 onset windows: the queue is always at the last step

https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/2026-ieee-big-data-traffic-flow-bench/discussion/742350

**Traffice_Flow_Bench_Organizer** (2026-09-21T16:26):

A participant reported a regularity in how the Task 2 windows were selected. It is real, we have checked it on all three splits, and this post hands it to everyone.

For windows marked queue_onset in window_index.csv, the timing of the answer is fixed. In all 120 of them the queue appears only at T+30, and the first five steps, T+5 through T+25, are empty. The cause is the window selector. It keeps the earliest origin whose 30-minute horizon contains a queue, and for a queue that begins at time t that origin is t minus 30 minutes, so the queue lands on the final step by construction.

queue_ongoing windows are not affected. That condition is defined by the 60 minutes of history already containing a queue, so a queue at the start of the horizon follows from the definition, and the history is published anyway.

We are leaving the data and the metric as they are. The property comes from the selection rule rather than from the values, it can be derived from the train split, which ships with its answers, and it is now public. Rebuilding the data a third time seven weeks before the deadline would cost everyone more than it is worth, and redefining S_queue to ignore time would turn Task 2 into a different task.

Using it is within the rules. You may condition on the condition column and place your predictions accordingly. Now that every team has been told, it is not an advantage held by anyone in particular.

One thing worth knowing: under a space-time IoU, spreading a prediction evenly across all six steps is expensive. What Task 2 still asks, and what will separate the leaderboard, is which links queue.

We would rather hand everyone the same information than let the ranking turn on who noticed.
