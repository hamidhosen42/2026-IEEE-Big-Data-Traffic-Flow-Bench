# Important update: award eligibility, final evaluation, and IEEE BigData 2026

https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/2026-ieee-big-data-traffic-flow-bench/discussion/744187

**Traffice_Flow_Bench_Organizer** (2026-09-28T22:40):

Dear TrafficFlowBench participants,

Thank you for your strong participation so far. More than 200 teams have already submitted. We are introducing the award procedure below to keep the competition fair for every participant, and so that we can reach winning teams quickly once the competition closes. The Evaluation section, the Timeline and Registration section and the Rules have been updated to match.

The leaderboard scoring rules are unchanged, and all submissions made on the rebuilt dataset remain valid. We are also working with Kaggle to remove the scores recorded before the dataset was rebuilt, and will update this thread once that is done.

1. Registration by e-mail is required for award eligibility
Send an e-mail to trafficflowbench@gmail.com with the subject line "Traffic Flow Bench Registration" by October 25, 2026, 23:59 AoE. Please include:


Your Kaggle team name

The Kaggle usernames, full names, affiliations and e-mail addresses of all team members

A primary contact person


If every member of your team is a K–12 student, please add "K–12 Team" to the subject line, for example "Traffic Flow Bench Registration (K–12 Team)".

Registration does not affect data access or leaderboard submissions, but only registered teams are eligible for awards. We use this information to contact winners and to prepare the results workshop.

2. Final package: code and report
By November 10, 2026, 23:59 AoE, teams seeking awards must send an e-mail with the subject line "Traffic Flow Bench Final Package – [Team Name]", including:


Code that reproduces the team's selected final Kaggle submission: a GitHub link or a zip file, with a README, environment details and a single entry script.

A PDF report describing your method, your analysis and your results across the four tasks. It should be complete and well organized. There is no required template and no page limit.


3. How awards are decided
The private leaderboard remains the main criterion. The highest-placed eligible teams on the private leaderboard are reviewed in order. The organizers run each team's code, and the reproduced result must match its selected final submission to within 1% of its private leaderboard score. A team that fails this check is not eligible, and the next eligible team is reviewed in its place.

For the teams that pass, the final award score is 85% private leaderboard and 15% report. Full details are in the Evaluation section.

4. K–12 Student Award
After discussion, the organizers decided that the K–12 Student Award (USD 500) is open to K–12 students only. Undergraduate students are not eligible for it. Every member of the team must be a currently enrolled K–12 student.

To award it, we go down the private leaderboard in order and give it to the highest-placed K–12 team that is eligible, passes the reproducibility check and has submitted a report. Participants under 18 need the consent of a parent or guardian, which we will collect before the award is confirmed.

5. Present your work at IEEE BigData 2026 in Phoenix
Winning and selected teams will be invited to present at IEEE BigData 2026, December 14 to 17, 2026, in Phoenix, Arizona, USA. Presenting is strongly encouraged but not required to receive a prize. Conference registration is separate from competition registration and is handled by the conference: https://bigdataieee.org/BigData2026/
