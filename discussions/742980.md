Notebook: 0.809 LB — physics-informed 4-task pipeline
Why physics instead of a transformer?
I considered it. But the scoring gives it away: Task 3 (physics consistency) isn't scored on its own — it grades your Task 1 output directly. Mess up conservation laws and you get dinged twice. Once I saw that, "just respect the physics" stopped being optional.
$$\mathcal{S}{total} = 0.35 \cdot \mathcal{S}{state} + 0.30 \cdot \mathcal{S}{queue} + 0.15 \cdot \mathcal{S}{physics} + 0.20 \cdot \mathcal{S}_{ODME}$$

Not claiming physics-first is universally correct — it just fit this particular metric structure.
What actually worked
1. Sorted stations by road topology, not sensor ID.
The dataset numbers detectors in whatever order someone typed them in — not how traffic actually flows. Congestion propagates upstream on a real freeway, not through a spreadsheet. Re-sorted using the corridor topology files instead. Interpolation got noticeably saner.
2. Soft Fundamental Diagram projection + 5-pass mass conservation (Tasks 1 & 3)
I project speed-flow estimates onto a calibrated Greenshields-type curve with a soft blend:
$$\hat{x}{proj} = (1 - \alpha) \cdot x{interp} + \alpha \cdot x_{FD}, \quad \alpha = 0.15$$
Translation: 85% trust the sensors, 15% gently peer-pressure the output into being physically plausible. Enough to kill the non-physical states without flattening the real signal. Add five passes of local flow conservation across adjacent links ($q_{down} \approx q_{up}$) and mass discrepancy in my validation set dropped ~74%.
3. Calibrated ODME regularization (Task 4) — the actual reason I broke 0.80
Task 4 is an underdetermined path flow problem: given link counts $c = Af$ and a seed prior $b$, solve:
$$\min_{f \ge 0} |Af - c|_2^2 + \lambda |f - b|_2^2$$

I was running $\lambda = 20.0$ early on — which tells the solver "ignore what you're actually observing and just trust the prior." Dropped it to $\lambda = 0.05$ to match the scale the benchmark implicitly expects, and $\mathcal{S}_{link}$ jumped from 0.987 to 0.9999 across all 10 corridor panels. This one number was responsible for nearly the whole 0.799 → 0.809 jump. Calibration bugs are humbling like that.
Dead ends (posting because negative results are underrated)


Ramp flux injection in Task 1: sounds right on paper — fold on/off-ramp readings into the mainline conservation balance. In practice the ramp loop detectors are noisy enough (dropouts, drift) that it made flow RMSE worse. Dropped it.

Treiber's Adaptive Smoothing Method: a genuinely well-established spatial filtering technique from traffic flow literature. It blurred exactly the sharp transitions at bottlenecks I needed to keep — speed error went from 3.88 to 10.81 km/h, and $\mathcal{S}_{state}$ dropped 0.147. Theoretically elegant, empirically painful here.


- Speed-based suppression for Task 2 onset prediction: assuming "110 km/h, no deceleration in the last 60 minutes" = safe from breakdown. Nope — capacity drops are frequently abrupt with almost no warning in the observation window. Onset IoU collapsed from 0.74 to 0.17.
