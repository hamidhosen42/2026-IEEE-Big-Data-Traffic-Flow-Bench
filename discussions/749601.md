Thank you for the earlier clarifications regarding Task 2's online forecasting requirements.

We understand that, for each forecast origin T, dynamic input data, model fitting, method selection, and threshold selection must respect the information available as of T. We also understand that earlier observations from the same split may be used for refitting, and that March data may inform April forecasts, but not vice versa.

We would appreciate clarification on how this requirement applies to the human model-development and selection process, particularly when multiple evaluation periods have already been inspected.

Consider the following scenario:


A forecasting method uses only the permitted historical observations available before each origin T. It does not access future observations, hidden labels, or post-origin metadata during inference.

All fitted parameters and thresholds are derived from training data preceding the evaluation period. The implementation and parameters are frozen before generating the relevant forecasts.

During earlier exploratory work, we had examined descriptive input-data characteristics (such as missingness, observation age, and coverage) from later evaluation periods. We may also have seen aggregate published window metadata, but did not use hidden queue labels or future observations as model inputs.

The decision to use the forecasting method is made after that exploratory analysis, even though the method itself can be reproduced using only pre-origin information.


Our questions are:
i) Does the as-of-origin restriction apply to the computational inputs, fitting, and selection procedures used to construct each forecast, or also to the researchers' prior exposure to descriptive information from later periods?

ii) If later-period input diagnostics have already been inspected, can a subsequently preregistered method still be used, provided its fitting, parameter selection, and predictions rely exclusively on permissible pre-origin data?

iii) What evidence or documentation would you expect to demonstrate compliance—for example, chronological training records, frozen configurations, source-data access logs, and per-origin prediction provenance?

We want to ensure that our model-development process, not just our inference implementation, complies with the intended evaluation protocol.
Thank you for your guidance.

**Traffice_Flow_Bench_Organizer** (2026-10-18T17:46):

Thank you for the careful question.

i) What you describe is fine. Missingness, observation age, coverage and the published window metadata are released for everyone to use, and looking at them is part of modeling the released data. The as-of-origin restriction concerns traffic observations: for a Task 2 window with origin T, no traffic data stamped after T may be used to produce that window's forecast, whether directly as an input or indirectly through fitting, selection or values written into the method.

ii) Yes. A method whose fitting, parameter selection and predictions rely only on data permitted for each origin may be used, regardless of what descriptive information was inspected earlier.

iii) No access logs or chronological records are required. In your report, explain the main design choices and the evidence behind them, for example results on the training split. At award review we will run your code, and for Task 2 we will rerun it with every row stamped after each window's origin removed and check that the predictions do not change.
